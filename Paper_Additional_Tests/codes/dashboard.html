Did anything leak?

This is the question that decides whether the rows above HGT mean anything. Two separate guarantees: the text is the same text the HGT reads, and no outcome vocabulary reaches the sparse models.

How much did the extra sanitizer actually change?

The HGT reads the cleaned-case text as-is. The flat-text models get one additional guard on top: 18,301 [LEAKAGE_MASK] artifacts and 379,251 operative-outcome term occurrences (3.21M characters) are stripped before vectorisation. If the TF-IDF advantage were leakage, removing all of it would collapse the number. It moves by at most 0.21 accuracy points — an order of magnitude smaller than the +2.50-point gap to HGT.

Where does the flat-text signal come from?

Same folds, same sanitizer, same Linear SVM as B3 — only the sections fed to TF-IDF change. This separates case-type base rates (preamble) from the case narrative (facts, arguments).

Reading. The preamble alone — party names, court and petition type, i.e. registry-level base rates rather than legal reasoning — reaches 74.45%, +13.61 points over the majority class. That looks like a shortcut worth worrying about, but it is almost entirely redundant with the narrative: adding the preamble on top of facts and arguments is worth only +1.56 points.

Facts and arguments on their own — every party name, court and petition type removed — reach 81.41%, already +0.77 against the HGT. So the flat-text advantage is not an identity shortcut. It comes from the case narrative, which is exactly the evidence the graph model is meant to reason over — which points at the frozen text encoder, not the graph, as the bottleneck.

How to read this

The graph is doing real work. Strip every edge and keep the same case features and the same trainer, and accuracy falls 2.94 points. That effect is the largest in the table and it is what the paper's representation claim rests on.

The heterogeneous attention is not what earns the number. A plain GAT on the type-collapsed graph — no node types, no per-relation weights, 36% fewer parameters — is statistically indistinguishable from HGT (p = 0.73). Relational GAT is slightly ahead of it. The accuracy-based argument for choosing HGT specifically does not survive; the explainability argument (typed attention is what makes the relation-level counterfactuals of §4.2 expressible) does.

A sparse lexical model beats the graph on this corpus. TF-IDF with a linear SVM reaches 83.13%. The likely reason is capacity where it matters: 300,000 explicit n-gram features retain lexical detail that a 1024-dimensional mean-pooled BGE-M3 section embedding averages away. This is reported as found, not tuned into or out of existence.

Entity counts alone are weak. Logistic Regression on the GNN's own 12 case scalars plus entity counts reaches only 60.20% — below the majority class. The structured metadata carries little signal without the text.
