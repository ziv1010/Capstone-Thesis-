Experiment 1

HGT Case-Embedding Extraction

After training, the penultimate-layer hidden representation for every case is extracted in a single forward pass. These embeddings are the substrate every downstream analysis reuses.

What

One forward pass over the trained HGT produces a dense vector per case node, plus its predicted probability, true label, split, and confidence. Saved as hgt_case_embeddings.npz.

Why

Re-running the model for every analysis is expensive. A frozen embedding lets clustering, nearest-neighbour search, and contrastive analyses run in seconds — and reveals what the model has internalized about each case.

How to Read

Confidence histogram = decisiveness. Split-wise accuracy = sanity check the saved predictions match a reasonable test/val/train story. Alignment row hints at whether embeddings track topology.

Key Findings

    Embedding Manifest

    Accuracy by Split

    Confidence Distribution

    Where the model lands on the probability axis — peaks at 0.9+ mean a decisive model.

    Patterns of Interest

    Embedding-space behaviour worth probing further.
    • If the confidence histogram is bimodal at 0.5 and 1.0, the model has a "sure or shrug" calibration — investigate the 0.5 cluster in the Case Explorer.
    • If train accuracy is much higher than test accuracy, embeddings memorize rather than generalize. Cross-check with Experiment 5 alignment.
    • If alignment NMI is low but accuracy is high, the HGT learned outcome-relevant signal beyond pure citation topology — that is the thesis claim.
    Experiment 2

    Counterfactual Evidence Masking

    For every case, evidence groups (specific nodes or whole relation types) are masked one at a time and the model is re-run. The resulting probability change Δp is the causal importance score.

    What

    Per case, build the L-hop receptive field, define evidence groups, mask each, record Δp, flip flag, attention score, and training-case support. Per-case CSVs are then aggregated by evidence type, relation type, and typed path.

    Why

    Attention says "the model looks at this neighbour"; counterfactuals say "removing this changes the outcome." Only the second answers the question a lawyer would ask. We compare the two to audit attention as an explanation tool.

    How to Read

    Total importance = cumulative |Δp| across cases. Flip rate = how often masking that group flips the predicted class. Attention overlap measures agreement between the two rankings. Identity rows (judge, court, parties) are leakage flags.

    Key Findings

      Evidence Type Importance

      Aggregate Σ|Δp| per legal evidence type across cases.

      Top Typed Paths

      Which relation chains carry the most causal weight.

      Relation-Type Importance

      Including rev_ (reverse) edges that exist only for HGT message passing.

      Identity-Leakage Sensitivity

      Judges, courts, parties. High importance here = audit alarm.

      Attention vs Counterfactual

      Top-k overlap. Low overlap = attention is unreliable as an explanation.

      Patterns of Interest

      Probing failure modes and surprises.
      • Which path families involve rev_ edges? Those rows mean the model needed the reverse message-passing signal — different from a real legal citation chain.
      • Identity-node mean importance > 0.05 with non-trivial flip rate is a flag: the model may be using who rather than what law.
      • If attention overlap is < 0.3, treat attention-only papers' findings with scepticism for this graph type.
      Identity Audit

      Identity Shortcut Test

      This audit tests whether normalized identity names alone predict held-out labels from train-split outcome priors, then compares that shortcut signal with the counterfactual flip-rate evidence.

      What

      For each identity type, estimate train-label priors by normalized name, score test cases using only identities seen in train, and report AUC, coverage, overlap, domain-baseline deltas, and permutation p-values.

      Why

      Counterfactual masking tells us the model is sensitive to identity nodes. This audit asks the sharper question: are those identities predictive even without legal text or graph context?

      How to Read

      High identity-only AUC plus improved log-loss over the domain baseline means shortcut risk. High flip rate but low identity-only AUC means sparse or context-specific model reliance.

        Frozen-model performance after removing identity nodes at inference time.

        Experiment 3

        Faithfulness Validation

        A counterfactual ranking is only useful if it is causally faithful. We sweep top-k masks under three rankings — counterfactual, attention, random — and measure how prediction probability evolves.

        What

        For each ranker, progressively (a) keep only top-k evidence (sufficiency) and (b) remove only top-k evidence (comprehensiveness). Record probability curves and AUCs per case.

        Why

        If the counterfactual ranking really captures causal influence, keeping top-k evidence should preserve the prediction fast and removing top-k should collapse it fast. Random is the floor; attention is the standard baseline.

        How to Read

        Higher sufficiency AUC = compact evidence preserves the prediction. Higher comprehensiveness AUC = top-k removal damages the prediction. The counterfactual line should sit visibly above attention and random on both.

        Key Findings

          AUC Comparison

          Counterfactual is the proposed ranker; attention and random are baselines.

          Sufficiency Curve

          Predicted-class probability vs k (keep only top-k).

          Comprehensiveness Curve

          Probability drop vs k (remove top-k).

          Prediction Buckets

          Are confident predictions also more explainable?

          Evidence Types per Bucket

          What kinds of evidence dominate at each confidence band.

          Patterns of Interest

          • If counterfactual sufficiency only marginally beats random, the per-case top-k is too small or evidence is genuinely diffuse.
          • If high-confidence wrong cases have high evidence purity, the model is not confused — it is being confidently misled by a clean-looking but misleading neighbourhood. Worth a deep dive in Case Explorer.
          • If evidence types differ between high-confidence-correct and high-confidence-wrong, the wrong cases may be driven by a specific suspect type (e.g. judges) — cross-check Experiment 2 leakage.
          Experiment 4

          Legal Communities (Structural)

          Cases are projected into a case-case graph weighted by rarity-adjusted shared authorities, then clustered with Leiden. Each community is a recurring legal-neighbourhood pattern.

          What

          Build a sparse case-case projection from case_feature_matrix (statutes, provisions, precedents, judges, courts) weighted by IDF. Run Leiden. Profile each community by size, dominant label, dominant domain bucket, and top authorities.

          Why

          If we are going to claim "this case looks like a familiar legal pattern", we need an explicit, corpus-grounded notion of "pattern". Communities give us that. They also surface failure communities — clusters where the model is confidently wrong.

          How to Read

          Largest communities reveal corpus structure. Failure communities (high high_confidence_wrong_n) are systematic blind spots. Top provisions/statutes identify the legal fingerprint of each pattern.

          Key Findings

            Largest Communities

            Click a row to drill into authorities and representative cases.

            Confident-Wrong Communities

            Where the model is wrong with high confidence — the audit-priority list.

            Domain Distribution

            Cases per domain bucket across all communities.

            Community Detail

            Profile, enriched evidence, representative cases.

            Patterns of Interest

            • Heavy single-domain communities (e.g. all motor accidents) confirm the case-case projection is doing reasonable topical clustering.
            • Mixed-domain communities (e.g. financial fraud + sexual offences) usually share a procedural authority like Section 482 CrPC — check the top provisions list.
            • Failure communities with dominant label ≠ dominant predicted label = the model contradicts the local legal consensus. Worth a manual case-by-case review.
            Experiment 5

            Embedding Cluster Characterization

            HDBSCAN clusters the HGT case embeddings. The resulting partition is then compared to the structural Leiden partition (Experiment 4) using ARI, NMI, V-measure.

            What

            Density-based clustering of HGT embeddings → clusters + a noise pile. Then cross-tabulate with structural communities. Per-cluster profile: size, dominant label/domain, dominant matching community.

            Why

            This answers: did the GNN actually use graph structure, or did it learn something else? High alignment ⇒ the model is mostly doing topology-aware classification. Low alignment with high accuracy ⇒ the model has found outcome-relevant signal beyond pure structure.

            How to Read

            Read alignment first (NMI, ARI). Then look for outcome-pure clusters (one label dominates) — those are the places the model carved out its own outcome-relevant geometry.

            Key Findings

              Community → Embedding Cluster Flow

              Leiden structural communities on the left; HDBSCAN embedding clusters on the right. Thick flows show where cases move.

              Alignment Metrics

              Embedding partition vs structural partition.

              Embedding Cluster Profiles

              One row per HDBSCAN cluster (plus the noise pile).

              How Structural Communities Split into Embedding Clusters

              High split count = a single legal-neighbourhood is being subdivided by HGT geometry.

              Patterns of Interest

              • Communities with many embedding clusters = the HGT is splitting a legal-neighbourhood into outcome-relevant subregions. Read these as model-discovered fine-grained patterns.
              • Embedding clusters that span many structural communities = the HGT collapsed across legal-neighbourhood boundaries — outcome similarity overrides citation similarity.
              • Pure (≥ 90% one label) clusters of n ≥ 50 are strong candidates for "this is what the model has learned to confidently classify."
              Experiment 6

              Counterfactual Neighborhoods (Similar & Opposite Cases)

              For a chosen case, find the cases the model sees as most alike in HGT-embedding space — the closest ones decided the same way and the closest ones decided the opposite way. The evidence differences become the contrastive explanation.

              What

              Cosine search over the frozen case embeddings. First a similarity network placing the case among its nearest same-label and opposite-label neighbours, then two contrast diagrams — closest agreeing case, closest disagreeing case — with feature differences ranked either by counterfactual effect or by IDF and label-discriminative skew.

              Why

              This is the "find me the most similar case that was decided the other way" question — exactly the kind of reasoning lawyers and judges already do from precedent. It complements Exp 2 (which evidence drives this prediction) with: which evidence isn't there compared to the closest opposite outcome.

              How to Read

              High cosine = the model sees the cases as similar despite opposite labels. Query-only features = present here, absent in the other case. Each evidence box carries its counterfactual rank: ▲ supports the decision (masking it lowers confidence), ▼ argues against it, and a ribbon marks a top-3 driving factor.

                Experiment 7

                Full-Graph Leiden Communities

                Leiden runs directly on the heterogeneous citation graph at multiple resolutions. Each community is a structurally coherent legal "neighbourhood" — cases that cite overlapping statutes, provisions and precedents.

                What you're looking at

                Pick a resolution below. Lower γ → broader legal domains; higher γ → sub-themes. Each card represents one community; the bars inside show its label balance and the model's prediction quality on that community.

                Key Findings

                  Resolution Sweep

                  Each row is one Leiden run. Higher resolution = more, smaller communities.

                  Largest Communities — visual cards

                  Hover a card to highlight. Bars show label distribution (green=label 1, red=label −1) and prediction quality (high-conf correct / wrong, low-conf correct / wrong).

                  Confident-Wrong Communities — audit priority

                  These communities have the most cases the model got wrong with high confidence. Investigate the dominant authorities.

                  Boundary Cases

                  Cases whose authority neighbours span multiple communities — intrinsically mixed legal context. Click a row to open the case.

                  How to read this tab

                  • Stack bar — label distribution: tells you whether a community is internally consistent on outcome. Roughly 50/50 means the legal pattern alone does not predict outcome.
                  • Stack bar — prediction quality: green (HC correct) is what we want; orange (HC wrong) is the real concern, that is the model being confidently wrong on a coherent legal pattern.
                  • Top authorities: the legal "fingerprint" of the community. Communities sharing a dominant statute usually share the procedural posture.
                  • Boundary cases: when a case cites authorities from two different communities, the model has less internal consensus to lean on.
                  Experiment 8

                  Community Hierarchy (Broad → Specific)

                  As the resolution parameter γ rises, broad legal communities split into more specific sub-themes. This tab shows that lineage so you can read it as a tree.

                  What you're looking at

                    Experiment 9

                    Bridge / Hub / Core Authorities

                    Every statute, provision and precedent is given a structural role based on the spread of cases that cite it. The four roles answer: does this authority anchor one legal niche, bridge a few, or knit many together?

                    What the four roles mean

                    CORE — almost all citing cases live in a single community (≥80% concentration). The authority anchors one legal niche.

                    BRIDGE — citing cases concentrate in a small handful of communities (typically 2). The authority is a transfer path between those niches.

                    HUB — citing cases spread across many communities, no dominant home. These are the structural backbone — the few authorities that knit the corpus together.

                    RARE — too few citing cases to classify. Set aside.
